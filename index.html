Why This Matters

Public transport is the backbone of urban mobility in Malaysia. Let's start with the big picture — who carries the most passengers, and how has ridership changed since 2019?

Chart 1
Average Daily Ridership by Service (2024)
Rapid Rail dominates — the combined LRT, MRT and Monorail network carries nearly twice as many daily passengers as Rapid Bus KL, the second-largest service.
Chart 2
Annual Ridership Trend, 2019–2024 (millions of trips)
COVID-19 MCO in April 2020 caused a ~70% collapse in ridership. By 2024, rail ridership is within striking distance of pre-pandemic records.

National Patterns

All services follow the same macro story — collapse, slow recovery, then acceleration. But patterns differ: MRT Kajang has overtaken LRT Kelana Jaya as the network's growth leader, and weekday demand is consistently higher across the board.

Chart 3 — Small Multiples
Monthly Ridership Index by Service, Jan 2019 – Dec 2024  (Jan 2019 = 100; ● marks COVID low point, end dot = Dec 2024 index)
MRT Kajang (green) has overtaken all other services in growth, now tracking well above 200% of its 2019 baseline. This reflects rapid urban expansion in the Kajang–Cyberjaya–Putrajaya corridor.
Chart 4

KTM Komuter shows the highest weekday premium (+71%) — clearly driven by work commutes between suburbs and KL Sentral. BRT Sunway's smaller gap reflects its mixed catchment of university students and shoppers.

Inside the Network

Origin–destination data reveals how the network is really used — which corridors generate the most journeys, and how flows concentrate around the core interchange at KL Sentral.

Chart 7 · Interactive — hover for trip counts
OD Heatmap: KTM Komuter Average Daily Trips Between Station Pairs (2024)
The brightest row belongs to KL Sentral — the dominant origin and destination on the Komuter network. KL Sentral ↔ Kepong Sentral is the single busiest corridor at 8,200 daily trips.
Chart 8
BRT Sunway Line — Station Boardings & Top Origin–Destination Flows (2024)
The route strip (top) shows stations sized by daily boardings. The ranked bars show the 7 busiest OD pairs — orange = outbound toward USJ7, blue = return toward Setia Jaya.

Daily Commuting Rhythm

When do people travel? The hourly rhythm of KTM Komuter reveals classic dual-peak commuter behaviour — sharp morning and evening spikes on weekdays, replaced by a smooth mid-day arc at weekends.

Chart 9 · Interactive — toggle weekday / weekend
KTM Komuter Hourly Ridership Profile (Typical Day)
Weekday: twin peaks at 7–8 AM (morning rush) and 5–6 PM (evening rush). The morning spike is sharper, reflecting staggered school and office start times.
Chart 10
Top 5 KTM Komuter Stations — Daily Ridership & Hourly Pattern
#StationDaily avgPattern (5 AM–10 PM)
KL Sentral dwarfs all others — handling nearly 3.5× more daily passengers than second-place Kepong Sentral, reflecting its role as the national interchange hub and KLIA gateway.

What This Tells Us

Three defining patterns — and the complete picture of Malaysian public transport modal share.

Chart 11 — Modal Share
Share of Daily Public Transport Trips by Mode (2024)
Rapid Rail accounts for 48% of all daily public transport trips — nearly half — despite serving only the Klang Valley. This underlines the critical importance of continued rail investment and capacity expansion.
01
Rail carries the largest share
Rapid Rail (LRT/MRT/Monorail) accounts for nearly half of all public transport trips in Malaysia, underlining the importance of continued rail investment.
02
Weekday demand dominates
Across all services, weekday ridership is 35–71% higher than weekends. Work-trip dependency means off-peak and weekend service capacity remains significantly underutilised.
03
Flows cluster around interchanges
OD data shows that trips concentrate around KL Sentral, Kepong Sentral and Subang Jaya — major interchange nodes — following classic twin-peak commuting patterns on weekdays.
